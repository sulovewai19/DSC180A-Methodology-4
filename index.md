## 1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?

The most interesting topic I learned about this quarter is the welfare surface framework and how machine learning can help governments make better resource allocation decisions. I found it interesting that having a more accurate prediction model does not always mean a program will achieve better outcomes. Sometimes, spending money to reach more people can be more beneficial than improving the model. Through our Quarter 1 project, I learned how proxy means testing (PMT) can predict household consumption and how different welfare functions can affect which households should receive assistance.

## 2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.

For Quarter 2, I am interested in exploring how machine learning and welfare-based resource allocation can help improve access to healthcare in underserved communities. I would like to investigate how mobile healthcare clinics could be placed in different locations to reach people who have limited access to medical services.

I would use machine learning to predict which communities have the greatest healthcare needs based on factors such as income, population, and access to existing healthcare facilities. Then, I would apply the welfare surface framework to compare different scenarios, such as opening more mobile clinics versus investing in better data to identify communities that need healthcare services the most. The goal would be to understand how limited healthcare resources can be allocated more effectively to benefit communities that need them the most.

## 3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?

One change I would consider making is testing different prediction models instead of relying mainly on LASSO regression for proxy means testing. For example, I would like to compare LASSO with random forests or gradient boosting to see whether more flexible models improve prediction accuracy.

I would also like to examine whether those improvements actually lead to better welfare outcomes. A model might predict household needs more accurately, but that does not necessarily mean it is the best choice when the cost of collecting data and implementing the program is considered.

## 4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?

I would be interested in learning more about geospatial analysis and optimization techniques. For a mobile healthcare clinic project, geospatial analysis could help identify underserved areas and measure how far communities are from existing healthcare facilities.

I would also like to explore clustering methods to group communities with similar needs and optimization algorithms to determine the best clinic locations under a limited budget. Combining these techniques with machine learning and welfare analysis could help us compare different resource allocation strategies and understand which approaches benefit the most people.
